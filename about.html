About me

Background

Ian Simpson was born in San Diego into a secular/liberal xtian family. He had a hard time finding purpose while he was a child. This led to a sense of being lost and to a certain extent despair. Alongside failure in school, family issues, and minor health issues, he found this overwhelming. When looking for role models he saw society looks up to those that seem superficially happy and carefree such as rappers or hippies. In following these bad examples, his pursuits took a further downturn.

After he caused himself many problems, he finally got the idea that he was looking for purpose in the wrong place. Along the way, HASHEM gifted him some talents. So, he saw life looking up, and investigated self-help, philosophy, and trades. However, it was still missing something. One day in community college philosophy class the professor introduced an idea that if the atheists were Heaven forbid right and everything is simply materialist and physical, then there would still be no downside to following a religion and believing in something. However, if a religion is right and one does not follow it there would be a serious problem.

Therefore, Ian investigated the far eastern religions that were popular in the San Diego region due to its "surfer/hippy" culture. However they seemed foolish or shallow, so he continued with an agnostic/self-help mindset. After a while he finished quite a few of his books and saw he had on the shelf a new international version bible that his grandmother sent him. He started in the first pages, as one does with other books, and found himself in a badly translated and manipulated version of the Sefer Beresheet. He learned about Adam, Eve, Noah, Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, The Twelve Tribes, and many other significant figures. As he learned he felt an immense connection to the historical, geographical, and scientific proofs; realistic and powerful stories; and awesome beauty of the Holy Torah. Continuing through the Five Books of Moses, he encountered the mighty verses that could not be hampered by the poor translation and lack of commentary.

Conclusion

Then he was free to investigate scriptural and philosophical sources of the three main religions in the world. The outcome of that research was: only Orthodox Judaism contains the Truth. He discovered Rabbis Yosef Mizrahi, Tovia Singer, Yaron Reuven, and Yehoshua Zitron. Through their lectures based on science, logical, and ancient Divine Information his choice was constantly strengthened. After about 2 more years he was introduced to Rabbi Lev Leib Lerner and continues to learn from these wonderful teachers to this day. Career Background and Aspirations

Music

Music was one of Ian's first passions and he spent much time listening to a variety growing up. In high school, Ian learned basics of guitar, as well as electronic music with FL Studio. In Senior year of high school, Ian released an album, showing ability to turn vague ideas into a concrete product. As he discovered the Holy Torah, music has taken on a different role in his life. He now understands that music is a powerful vessel meant for the service of G-d and not for things that are contrary to that goal.

Automotive

Ian started off doing basic service at his residence. He then moved to taking college classes, with a main focus on 12v electrical systems and their applications. Additionally he learned in depth engine technology including disassembling a Honda B18A1 and watching a coworker replace a Honda IMA system. His jobs included independent, dealership, and ev oem roles. Along the way he performed personal research to better understand the product, furthering electrical, PCM, and suspension knowledge. After that he was in a Service Advisor/Sales role, where he excelled in tire sales.

Computer science

During his time as a Service Advisor he took interest in Computer Science. He joined a variety of classes during the time he was a full-time student. His coursework included: a sudoku checker in JavaScript, an expense tracker in C++, and a 2d sidescrolling video game with complex collision detection logic using the p5.js library. After passing exercises on websites like LeetCode, he started focusing on web developmentent and enterprise technology (particularly in the Salesforce ecosystem). A recent project was completed to analyze his driving business for cost reduction purposes. This took the form of a C++ command line app that tracked how much of his total earnings he was spending on gas over time. Since Ian has transitioned his focus from C++ and low-level programming to web and AI he ported this app to a web page format. Another reason for the port was ease of use across different platforms, as C++ compilers are not automatically installed on all machines.

Uber tips:

    Greet customers but try to avoid speaking to them as much as possible. Rabban Yohanan ben Zakkai was known to greet every man first, even gentiles. Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel said in Masekhet Avot (Ch. 1) "Kol hamarbeh devarim mevi het": "anyone who speaks excessively brings on sin". We saw instances of this when we were friendly with customers and they either tried to convince us of their false ideologies or made other inappropriate comments. I learned to keep a professional, courteous attitude, similar to a chauffeur.
    If customers are late to pickup or speak to you disrespectfully do not become upset. Rabbi Eliezer said in Masekhet Avot(Ch. 2) "do not be easy to anger" and it is further taught in Ch. 5 that someone who is easy to anger and hard to calm is a wicked, while someone who is hard to anger and easy to calm is considered a pious person. The Ramban writes extensively about anger in his famous Iggeret, particularly quoting the Gemara in Nedarim 22a as follows" "Anyone who gets angry all types of hell reign over him" as it is written "remove anger from your and remove evil from your flesh (Kohelet 11:10)" and there is no evil besides hell as it is said "and also the wicked is for the day of evil"(Mishlei 16:4)". Also HASHEM controls your parnassah(livelihood) as we learned from Rabbi Yaron Reuven shlita and the Artscroll Iggeret HaGra by Rabbi Shai Graucher in the name of the Talmud in Tractates Rosh HaShana and Betzah (both page 16). No one can take from you unless He wants them to, Heaven forbid.
